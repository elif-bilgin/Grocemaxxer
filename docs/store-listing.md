# Play Store listing copy

Paste-ready text for the Play Console listing. Character limits are Play's.

## App name (30 characters max)

```
GroceMaxxer
```

## Short description (80 characters max)

```
Your grocery run, optimized — items sorted into one walk through your store.
```

## Full description (4000 characters max)

```
GroceMaxxer turns your shopping list into a route.

Type in what you need and GroceMaxxer groups the items by the part of the
store they live in, then orders those groups into one sensible walk — produce
near the entrance, frozen just before the checkout, and so on. Check things
off in the order they appear and you shouldn't have to double back.

PICK YOUR STORE
Every supermarket is laid out differently, so the walking order changes with
it. GroceMaxxer ships layouts modelled on several large chains — among them
Safeway, Whole Foods, Target and Trader Joe's — each ordered to follow that
shop's typical path, including the cart-packing rules of thumb: heavy dry
goods first, produce on top of them, delicate bakery items last. No store
selected? You get a sensible generic route.

BUILT TO BE FAST TO USE
- Start typing and suggestions appear from a built-in catalogue of around 250
  common grocery items, already filed under the right section.
- Not sure what to call it? Browse by aisle and tap to add.
- Duplicates are skipped automatically and names are tidied up as you type.
- Got the category wrong? Override it when you add the item.

CHECKING OFF FEELS GOOD
Checked items sink to the bottom of their section, and a section you've
finished folds away into a tidy stack at the bottom of the screen. Clear the
last item and the whole list celebrates with you.

YOUR LISTS, BY DAY
Lists are kept per date. Start a fresh one, pick up where you left off, or
pull up the list from any earlier shopping day — it gets copied to today, so
your history stays intact.

SHARE A LIST WITH WHOEVER IS SHOPPING
Send your list as a plain, readable message through any app you already use.
When someone sends you one, GroceMaxxer spots it on your clipboard, shows you
exactly how it differs from your list, and lets you overwrite, merge item by
item, or ignore it.

MAKE IT YOURS
Four pastel accent colours and a dark mode.

NO ACCOUNT, NO INTERNET, NO ADS
GroceMaxxer has no sign-in, asks for no permissions, and has no internet
access at all. Everything lives on your phone. It's free, it has no adverts,
and it collects nothing about you — there is nothing to collect it with.

GroceMaxxer is an independent app. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or
sponsored by any retailer named above. All trademarks are the property of
their respective owners. The store layouts are the developer's own
observations of how those shops are typically arranged, not official data
from the retailers.
```

## Notes

- The em dash in the short description is intentional; Play accepts it.
- **Naming the supermarkets: how this stays inside policy.** Play's
  intellectual-property rule is not a ban on mentioning a brand. The test is
  whether the use could confuse a user about the source of the product — who
  made the app, and whether the retailer is behind it. Rejections for
  "unauthorized use of brand or trademark" are about implied affiliation, not
  about the word appearing. Four things keep this listing on the right side
  of that line, and all four have to hold:

  1. **No brand in the app name, developer name or short description.** Those
     are the fields a user reads as identity. GroceMaxxer's are clean.
  2. **Named as examples, not as a billing.** "layouts modelled on several
     large chains — among them …" describes a feature. "The list app for
     Safeway" would claim a relationship. Never use "for <brand>",
     "official", "powered by" or a brand as a section heading.
  3. **An explicit non-affiliation paragraph**, which is the last block of
     the full description. It is not a documented safe harbour, but the
     policy turns on confusion, so saying plainly that there is no
     relationship goes directly to the test. It also disclaims the second
     confusion risk: that the layouts are official store data. They are not —
     they are observations, and the description now says so.
  4. **No brand assets anywhere in the graphics.** No logos, no store colour
     schemes, no storefront photography in the icon, feature graphic or
     screenshots, and no brand name used as a screenshot caption. A
     screenshot showing the in-app store selector is fine — it depicts the
     app's own interface — but do not make a brand name the focal point of
     one.

  Mentioning each name once, as this description does, also avoids reading as
  keyword stuffing, which is a separate metadata violation.

  If a rejection does arrive, the remedy is to drop the names from the
  listing and keep them in the app, where they label a setting rather than
  advertise anything.

- "around 250" tracks `PresetCatalog.items`; update it if the catalogue grows
  substantially.
